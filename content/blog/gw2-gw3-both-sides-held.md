Two gameweeks since the last note. Both entered sides went in unchanged for GW2 and again for GW3. Bruno kept the armband in both. No chips. Free transfers rolled each week.

Short version: after three gameweeks the two sides are level. Haaland-in 180, no-Haaland 181. The T-48h packet had the no-Haaland robust arm 21 points ahead on its six-gameweek score (255.88 against 234.32). Three weeks in, that gap is one point, and it's gone the other way on the last two.

## The retain decision

The 27 Aug hold briefing named the falsifiers that would move the Haaland-in side before the GW2 deadline. None of them fired on Friday. Gibbs-White was in Forest's XI, Kinsky kept the Spurs gloves over Dubravka, and there was no fresher packet to rescore against. So the free transfer was banked, as the briefing said it would be.

Into GW3 the same logic applied and the loop held again. Two free transfers in hand, nothing on the official injury feed touching the XI, and the packet still degraded. The agent's line has been that transfers are for falsified positions, not for chasing last week's points. Xhaka's 9 sitting on the bench in GW1 was noise under that rule; it went 6 and 3 in the two weeks since.

That means three free transfers banked into GW4 (deadline Saturday 12 Sep, 13:30 UK). Whether that is discipline or inertia is the question I'll come back to at the end.

On the no-Haaland side the equivalent list was never written down because that arm came straight from the optimiser rather than the strategy agent. But two of its 11 have stopped playing (Senesi, Beto), and the bench has carried it for two weeks. Beto out is not a judgement call any more.

## What I take from the models so far

The captain pick has been the one place where the packet numbers were used directly and it is net positive: Bruno over Haaland gained 20 in GW2 and lost 7 in GW3. In GW1 it was a wash (both scored 2).

The hold-unless-falsified rule has held the Haaland-in side steady through a 29 and a 91 without a reactive move either way. That is what it was meant to do. Where it is weaker is that the falsifier list only covers the advisory side and only covers what the agent could see at T-24h. Wilson's minutes went the wrong way over three weeks; the trigger was written as a single-week collapse.

The six-gameweek comparator score put the no-Haaland arm 21 points clear. Three weeks in, it isn't. That is too small a sample to say anything about the scorer, but it is exactly why I entered both.
